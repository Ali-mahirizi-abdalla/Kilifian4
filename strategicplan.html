PART 1: STRATEGIC FOUNDATION

1. Executive Summary

Kilifian4 Integrity and Sustainability Initiative (KISI) is a community-based organisation registered in Kilifi County, Kenya, in 2025. KISI is dedicated to promoting sustainable livelihoods, environmental conservation, and community health through integrity-driven, locally appropriate innovations.

This Strategic Plan 2026–2031 sets out KISI's vision, mission, core values, strategic pillars, implementation framework, monitoring and evaluation systems, budget, and sustainability strategy for the next five years. The plan is guided by KISI's motto, "Keep It Sustainable and Sound" (KISS), and is aligned with Kenya's Vision 2030, the Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda (BETA), the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the Commonwealth Foundation's strategic priorities, and the Kilifi County Integrated Development Plan (CIDP) 2023–2027.

Key Highlights:

  • Vision: A global network of thriving, equitable, and resilient communities.
  • Mission: To empower individuals, organisations, and governments to build sustainable societies.
  • Strategic Pillars: Eight interconnected pillars.
  • Target Beneficiaries: 10,000 households across Kilifi County by 2031.
  • Total Budget: GBP 500,000 over five years.
  • Funding Sources: Grants, donations, social enterprise revenue, and government partnerships.
  • Governance: A seven-member Management Committee.
  • M&E: A comprehensive framework with 8 impact indicators, 40 outcome indicators, and 150 output indicators.
  • Risk Management: A structured Risk Register with 20 identified risks and mitigation strategies.
  • Sustainability: Social enterprise revenue, reserves, and diversified funding.

2. Introduction and Background

2.1 About KISI

Kilifian4 Integrity and Sustainability Initiative (KISI) is a non-political, non-denominational membership association registered in Kilifi County, Kenya. KISI was founded in 2025 by a group of professionals from Pwani University and its structured affiliates.

2.2 Rationale for the Strategic Plan

Kilifi County faces significant challenges:

  • Poverty: 71.7% of the population lives below the poverty line (KNBS, 2023)
  • Unemployment: Youth unemployment exceeds 40%
  • Water Scarcity: 56% of Kenyans lack access to safe water (WHO, 2024)
  • Energy Poverty: 92% of households lack reliable energy access
  • Food Insecurity: 65% of households are food insecure
  • Environmental Degradation: Deforestation, soil erosion, climate change
  • Gender Inequality: Women have limited access to education, income, decision-making
  • Health Challenges: Oral health issues, waterborne diseases, limited healthcare

2.3 Development Process

This Strategic Plan was developed through a participatory process involving the KISI Management Committee, KISI members, community representatives, local government officials, partner organisations, and beneficiaries.

2.4 Alignment with National and International Frameworks

  • Kenya Vision 2030

  • Kilifi County Integrated Development Plan (CIDP) 2023–2027
  • Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
  • Africa Union Agenda 2063

6. Strategic Pillars and Objectives

Pillar 1: Digital Libraries and Information Literacy

Budget: GBP 50,000 | Responsible: Digital Literacy Lead

Objectives:

  • Establish and maintain a network of accessible digital libraries.
  • Develop and deliver training programmes on digital literacy.
  • Foster a culture of self-directed learning.

Targets (2026–2031): 5 digital libraries established; 5,000 individuals trained in digital literacy; 100 community information literacy workshops conducted; 1 digital repository with 10,000+ resources.

Pillar 2: Smart (LED) Agriculture for Smallholder Farmers

Budget: GBP 60,000 | Responsible: Agriculture Lead

Objectives:

  • Promote adoption of energy-efficient LED lighting.
  • Provide training on sustainable water and nutrient management.
  • Increase crop yield while reducing environmental impact.

Targets: 500 farmers trained; 100 LED kits distributed; 30% average yield increase; 50 demonstration farms established.

Pillar 3: Crop Research and Feasibility Lab

Budget: GBP 70,000 | Responsible: Research Lead

Objectives: Research and develop resilient crop varieties; test soil and climate conditions; bridge research and practical farming.

Targets: 1 research lab; 10 crop varieties tested; 500 farmers advised; 5 research publications.

Pillar 4: Innovation and Incubation

Budget: GBP 80,000 | Responsible: Innovation Lead

Objectives: Provide mentorship, funding, and resources to entrepreneurs; facilitate connections between innovators and markets; promote value addition.

Targets: 50 startups incubated; 100 entrepreneurs trained; 20 value chains developed; 200 jobs created.

Pillar 5: Health, Water, and Green Energy

Budget: GBP 100,000 | Responsible: Health, Water, and Energy Lead

Objectives: Improve access to clean water and sanitation; promote preventative healthcare; deploy affordable green energy solutions.

Targets: 10,000 households access clean water; 20 water harvesting systems installed; 1,000 households adopt green energy; 50% reduction in waterborne diseases.

Pillar 6: Women, Young Mothers, and Children

Budget: GBP 60,000 | Responsible: Gender and Youth Lead

Objectives: Provide educational support and scholarships; offer vocational training and financial literacy; promote gender equality.

Targets: 1,000 women trained; 500 young mothers supported; 200 scholarships awarded; 50% increase in women's leadership roles.

Pillar 7: Care for the Ageing

Budget: GBP 30,000 | Responsible: Ageing Lead

Objectives: Establish community-based day care centres; run mental wellness programmes; advocate for elderly rights.

Targets: 5 elderly support centres; 1,000 elderly persons supported; 50 advocacy campaigns; 100 caregivers trained.

Pillar 8: Climate Resilience and E-Waste

Budget: GBP 50,000 | Responsible: Environment Lead

Objectives: Raise awareness on climate change; establish e-waste collection centres; promote afforestation and conservation.

Targets: 100,000 trees planted; 5 e-waste collection centres; 10,000 community members educated; 50% reduction in illegal e-waste disposal.

14. M&E Framework

14.1 Purpose of M&E

The M&E framework ensures that KISI tracks progress, measures impact, identifies challenges, ensures accountability, and documents lessons learned.

14.2 M&E Principles

  • Results-based
  • Participatory
  • Gender-sensitive
  • Utilisation-focused
  • Ethical and safe
  • Integrated into all activities

14.3 M&E Structure

Level Responsibility
Management Committee Overall oversight; approve M&E plan; review reports
M&E Officer Design M&E tools; collect and analyse data; report
Pillar Leads Collect output data; support outcome monitoring
Community Mobilisers Collect field data; community feedback
Beneficiaries Participate in data collection; provide feedback
External Evaluators Conduct independent evaluations

14.4 M&E Processes

  1. Baseline survey (Year 1)
  2. Monthly data collection and reporting
  3. Quarterly review meetings
  4. Mid-year evaluations
  5. Annual reviews
  6. Mid-term evaluation (Year 3)
  7. End-term evaluation (Year 5)
  8. Learning events and documentation

14.5 Data Management

  • Data collection tools: surveys, interviews, focus groups, observation, records review.
  • Data storage: secure electronic and physical systems.
  • Data quality: regular verification, triangulation, and audits.
  • Data protection: compliance with Kenya Data Protection Act 2019.
  • Data sharing: only with consent; anonymised for reporting.

14.6 Reporting

  • Monthly progress reports (internal).
  • Quarterly financial and programmatic reports (donors).
  • Annual reports (AGM, donors, stakeholders).
  • Evaluation reports (mid-term, end-term).
  • Learning briefs and case studies.

17. Risk Management Framework

17.1 Approach

KISI adopts a proactive risk management approach that identifies, assesses, mitigates, monitors, and reviews risks continuously. The Risk Register is maintained by the Finance Officer and reviewed quarterly by the Management Committee.

24. Sustainability Strategy

24.1 Sustainability Pillars

Pillar Strategy
Financial Social enterprise revenue; reserves; diversified funding
Technical Trained community trainers; knowledge transfer; documentation
Institutional Strong governance; policies; systems; leadership development
Environmental Circular economy; renewable energy; conservation
Community Ownership; participation; local leadership
Partnership Strategic alliances; MoUs; co-funding

24.2 Financial Sustainability

  • Social enterprise revenue: Neem paste, BSF larvae, chicken feed, water kiosks.
  • Target: GBP 150,000 over 5 years.
  • Reserves: Build reserve fund equal to 6 months of operating costs.
  • Diversified funding: Grants, donations, government contracts, earned income.
  • Cost recovery: Charge fees for training, consultancy, and services.

24.3 Technical Sustainability

  • Train community trainers who can pass on knowledge.
  • Document all processes and SOPs.
  • Establish demonstration sites.
  • Maintain equipment and infrastructure.
  • Foster peer-to-peer learning.

24.4 Institutional Sustainability

  • Strong governance structure.
  • Clear policies and procedures.
  • Financial management systems.
  • M&E systems.
  • Leadership development and succession planning.
  • Membership growth and engagement.

24.5 Community Sustainability

  • Community ownership and participation.
  • Local leadership in project management.
  • Community contributions (in-kind and financial).
  • Sustainability committees for each project.
  • Integration into local development plans.

24.6 Environmental Sustainability

  • Circular economy principles.
  • Renewable energy use.
  • Water conservation.
  • Waste reduction and recycling.
  • Tree planting and conservation.

24.7 Partnership Sustainability

  • Formal MoUs with key partners.
  • Joint planning and implementation.
  • Co-funding arrangements.
  • Shared M&E and learning.
  • Regular partner forums.

25. Exit Strategy

25.1 Exit Principles

  • Sustainability first
  • Community ownership
  • No abrupt withdrawal
  • Gradual transition
  • Documentation and learning

25.2 Exit Phases

Phase Timing Activities
Preparation Year 4 Assess sustainability; strengthen community capacity
Transition Year 5 Gradually hand over to community structures
Exit End of Year 5 Formal handover; final evaluation; documentation

25.3 Exit Criteria

  • Community structures fully operational.
  • Financial sustainability demonstrated.
  • Technical capacity in place.
  • M&E systems functional.
  • Partnerships formalised.
  • No unmet obligations.

25.4 Post-Exit Support

  • Light-touch mentoring for 1 year.
  • Occasional technical support as needed.
  • Documentation and learning sharing.
  • Continued partnership on specific initiatives.

25.5 Exit Risks and Mitigation

Risk Mitigation
Community unable to sustain Strengthen capacity; gradual transition
Funding shortfall Build reserves; diversify revenue
Loss of key staff Succession planning; documentation
Infrastructure breakdown Maintenance training; spare parts
Partner withdrawal Formal MoUs; diversified partners
