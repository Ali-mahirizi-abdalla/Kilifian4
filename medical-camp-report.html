MEDICAL CAMP REPORT: Disability Assessment and Rehabilitation Services

Samwel Wabuke & Team

Medical Students
Jul 9, 2026

Disability Assessment and Rehabilitation Services During a Medical Outreach at Marereni Dispensary, Malindi

Venue: Marereni Dispensary, Malindi Sub-County, Kilifi County, Kenya
Organizing Institution: World Friends
Date of Medical Camp: 9th July 2026
Participants:
  • Samwel Wabuke: HB01/PU/42710/24
  • Evelyne Adongo: HB01/PU/42701/24
  • Fredrick Mwita : HB01/PU/30373/24

Introduction

On 9th July 2026, we participated in a community medical outreach organized by World Friends at Marereni Dispensary in Malindi. The medical camp aimed to improve access to healthcare services among underserved members of the community by providing free medical consultations, rehabilitation services, health education, and referrals where necessary.

During the outreach, we were specifically attached to the Disability Assessment and Rehabilitation Department, where patients with physical disabilities, neurological disorders, musculoskeletal conditions, and developmental disorders were assessed and managed. This department played a vital role in identifying functional limitations, improving mobility, reducing pain, preventing disability progression, and enhancing patients' quality of life through physiotherapy and rehabilitative interventions.

The experience provided valuable practical exposure to disability assessment, neurological and musculoskeletal examination, rehabilitation techniques, multidisciplinary teamwork, and patient-centered care.

Objectives of the Medical Camp

The objectives of the medical outreach were to:

  • Improve access to quality healthcare services within the community.
  • Identify individuals living with disabilities and functional impairments.
  • Assess patients with neurological and musculoskeletal disorders.
  • Provide physiotherapy and rehabilitation services.
  • Relieve pain and improve functional independence.
  • Promote early identification and intervention for children with developmental delays.
  • Educate patients and caregivers on rehabilitation and home-based care.
  • Strengthen community awareness regarding disability prevention and management.
  • Provide practical clinical experience to healthcare trainees.

Department of Attachment

Throughout the medical outreach, we were stationed in the Disability Assessment and Rehabilitation Department. The department focused on evaluating patients with physical impairments and providing rehabilitation aimed at restoring function, reducing pain, preventing complications, and maximizing independence.

Patients were systematically assessed before individualized treatment plans were developed and implemented by the rehabilitation team.

Roles and Responsibilities

During the outreach, we actively participated under the supervision of qualified healthcare professionals. Our responsibilities included:

  1. Assisting in patient registration and documentation.
  2. Observing and participating in disability assessments.
  3. Assisting patients during physical examinations.
  4. Recording patient histories and presenting complaints.
  5. Observing neurological and musculoskeletal examinations.
  6. Assisting patients during physiotherapy sessions.
  7. Educating patients and caregivers on rehabilitation exercises.
  8. Observing the use of various physiotherapy modalities and rehabilitation equipment.
  9. Maintaining infection prevention and control measures.
  10. Ensuring respectful, ethical, and confidential patient care.

Conditions Encountered

The rehabilitation clinic received patients presenting with a wide range of neurological, musculoskeletal, and developmental conditions. Some of the common conditions encountered included:

  1. Low Back Pain: Many adult patients presented with chronic or acute low back pain, often associated with prolonged manual labour, poor posture, degenerative spinal conditions, or muscle strain. Assessment focused on pain characteristics, range of motion, posture, muscle tenderness, and functional limitations.
  2. Sciatica: Several patients complained of pain radiating from the lower back to the buttocks and lower limbs due to compression or irritation of the sciatic nerve. Clinical assessment involved evaluating pain distribution, muscle strength, sensation, reflexes, and gait.
  3. Right-Sided Hemiparalysis: Patients with right-sided hemiparalysis, commonly following stroke, demonstrated weakness or paralysis affecting the right upper and lower limbs. Rehabilitation emphasized improving muscle strength, balance, coordination, mobility, and performance of activities of daily living.
  4. Generalized Muscle Weakness: Patients with muscle weakness resulting from neurological disorders, prolonged illness, aging, or musculoskeletal conditions were assessed for muscle power, endurance, joint mobility, and functional capacity before individualized strengthening exercises were initiated.
  5. Epilepsy: Several patients living with epilepsy attended the clinic for assessment and follow-up. Although physiotherapy is not a direct treatment for epilepsy, rehabilitation focused on improving balance, coordination, mobility, muscle strength, and reducing complications associated with recurrent seizures or prolonged immobility.
  6. Disjointed or Malunited Fractures: Patients with previous fractures presented with persistent pain, joint stiffness, reduced range of motion, muscle wasting, deformities, or impaired function due to incomplete rehabilitation. Physiotherapy interventions aimed to restore mobility, increase muscle strength, and improve functional use of the affected limbs.
  7. Children with Developmental Delays and Physical Disabilities: One of the most remarkable observations involved children who had significantly delayed developmental milestones. Some children had reached 8 years of age yet had not achieved independent walking. Others presented with deformities or functional impairments affecting the upper limbs, making it difficult to perform normal daily activities.
    These children underwent comprehensive assessment, including evaluation of:
    • Gross motor development
    • Muscle tone
    • Muscle strength
    • Joint range of motion
    • Posture
    • Balance
    • Coordination
    • Functional mobility
    • Limb deformities
    Parents and caregivers were educated on the importance of continued physiotherapy, home exercise programs, regular follow-up, and early intervention to maximize developmental outcomes.

Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation Interventions Observed

Patients received individualized rehabilitation based on their diagnosis and functional limitations. The following interventions were observed:

Physiotherapy Exercises

Therapeutic exercises were prescribed to improve:

  • Muscle strength
  • Joint mobility
  • Flexibility
  • Balance
  • Coordination
  • Functional independence
  • Gait

Heat Therapy (Heat Modality)

Heat therapy was applied to patients with musculoskeletal pain and muscle stiffness. The therapeutic effects included:

  • Increased local blood circulation
  • Reduced muscle spasm
  • Pain relief
  • Improved tissue extensibility
  • Relaxation before exercise

Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS)

TENS was used primarily for pain management. Electrical impulses stimulated sensory nerves, helping to reduce pain through modulation of pain transmission and stimulation of endogenous pain-relieving mechanisms.

Electrical Muscle Stimulation (EMS)

EMS was applied to patients with muscle weakness and reduced muscle activation. It helped:

  • Stimulate muscle contraction
  • Prevent muscle atrophy
  • Improve muscle strength
  • Facilitate neuromuscular re-education
  • Enhance recovery following neurological injury

Percussion Massage Gun

The percussion massage gun was used for selected patients to:

  • Reduce muscle tightness
  • Improve local circulation
  • Decrease muscle soreness
  • Promote muscle relaxation
  • Prepare muscles before therapeutic exercise

Reflex Hammer

The reflex hammer was used during neurological examinations to assess deep tendon reflexes. Evaluation of these reflexes assisted clinicians in identifying abnormalities affecting the central or peripheral nervous system and guided diagnosis and rehabilitation planning.

Cranial Nerve Assessment

Cranial nerve assessment was performed for patients with neurological conditions, particularly those presenting with hemiparalysis, muscle weakness, and suspected neurological deficits. The following assessments were carried out:

  1. CN I – Olfactory nerve: Assessed sense of smell using familiar odors.
  2. CN II – Optic nerve: Tested visual acuity and visual fields.
  3. CN III – Oculomotor nerve: Assessed pupillary light reflex, pupil size, eyelid elevation, and most extraocular eye movements.
  4. CN IV – Trochlear nerve: Evaluated downward and inward eye movement.
  5. CN V – Trigeminal nerve: Tested facial sensation (ophthalmic, maxillary, and mandibular divisions) and muscles of mastication by asking the patient to clench the jaw.
  6. CN VI – Abducens nerve: Assessed lateral eye movement (abduction).
  7. CN VII – Facial nerve: Evaluated facial symmetry and movements by asking the patient to raise the eyebrows, close the eyes tightly, smile, puff out the cheeks, and show the teeth.
  8. CN VIII – Vestibulocochlear nerve: Assessed hearing using whispered voice or finger-rub tests and observed balance where appropriate.
  9. CN IX – Glossopharyngeal nerve: Assessed swallowing and the gag reflex (when indicated).
  10. CN X – Vagus nerve: Evaluated palate elevation, voice quality, swallowing, and uvula position while the patient said "Ah."
  11. CN XI – Accessory nerve: Tested shoulder shrug and head rotation against resistance to assess the trapezius and sternocleidomastoid muscles.
  12. CN XII – Hypoglossal nerve: Assessed tongue protrusion and side-to-side movement, observing for deviation, weakness, or muscle wasting.

These assessments helped identify neurological impairments, localize lesions, and guide appropriate physiotherapy and rehabilitation interventions.

Medications

Patients received medications according to their diagnoses and clinical needs. Commonly prescribed drugs included paracetamol and ibuprofen for pain and inflammation, muscle relaxants for muscle spasms, and antiepileptic medications such as carbamazepine or sodium valproate for patients with epilepsy. Patients with chronic conditions, including hypertension and diabetes, received appropriate medications or were referred for continued management. Some patients were also prescribed vitamin and mineral supplements, such as vitamin B complex and calcium, to support recovery. All medications were dispensed with instructions on the correct dosage, duration of treatment, adherence, and the importance of follow-up care.

Skills and Knowledge Acquired

The medical outreach enhanced our understanding and practical skills in:

  • Disability assessment
  • Musculoskeletal examination
  • Neurological examination
  • Assessment of muscle strength and tone
  • Recognition of developmental delays in children
  • Functional mobility assessment
  • Physiotherapy principles
  • Pain management techniques
  • Rehabilitation planning
  • Patient communication
  • Caregiver education
  • Professional ethics
  • Teamwork within a multidisciplinary rehabilitation team

Challenges Observed

Several challenges affected service delivery, including:

  • A high number of patients requiring rehabilitation services.
  • Limited rehabilitation equipment relative to patient demand.
  • Chronic conditions requiring long-term follow-up beyond the duration of the outreach.
  • Delayed presentation of children with developmental disabilities, reducing the potential benefits of early intervention.
  • Financial constraints limiting some patients' ability to continue rehabilitation after the outreach.

Recommendations

Based on our observations, we recommend that:

  1. Community medical rehabilitation outreaches should be conducted more frequently.
  2. Public awareness should be increased regarding the importance of early identification and management of disabilities.
  3. Parents should seek prompt assessment for children with delayed developmental milestones.
  4. Rehabilitation services should be strengthened at primary healthcare facilities.
  5. More physiotherapy equipment should be made available in rural health facilities.
  6. Follow-up programs should be established for patients requiring long-term rehabilitation.
  7. Community health workers should be trained to identify and refer individuals with disabilities early.

Conclusion

The medical outreach organized by World Friends at Marereni Dispensary on 9th July 2026 provided an invaluable opportunity to participate in disability assessment and rehabilitation services within a community setting. Working in the Disability Assessment and Rehabilitation Department exposed us to a broad spectrum of neurological, musculoskeletal, and developmental conditions affecting both adults and children.

The experience highlighted the importance of comprehensive assessment, individualized physiotherapy, and multidisciplinary rehabilitation in improving functional ability, reducing pain, and enhancing quality of life. It also underscored the need for early intervention, particularly among children with developmental delays, to optimize long-term outcomes.

Participation in this outreach strengthened our clinical knowledge, practical skills, communication abilities, and appreciation of the critical role rehabilitation services play in promoting independence and inclusion for persons living with disabilities.

Acknowledgements

We sincerely thank World Friends for organizing and supporting this impactful medical outreach. We are also grateful to the healthcare professionals and physiotherapists at Marereni Dispensary for their guidance, mentorship, and willingness to share their knowledge and clinical expertise throughout the exercise.

We further appreciate the patients and their caregivers for their cooperation and trust, which made this outreach a valuable learning experience. Finally, we acknowledge the dedication and teamwork of all healthcare workers whose commitment ensured the success of the medical camp.
